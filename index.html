Free IRL streaming to Twitch from an Android phone

Fullbars is open source and streams straight to Twitch from your phone. Add a small server you rent, and it can send the stream over mobile data, a Wi‑Fi hotspot and spare phones at once. When every connection drops, viewers see your Signal lost card instead of a frozen stream.

Download the test build How to set it up

Version 0.8.0, for Android 12 and up. It's a test build on GitHub and isn't on Google Play yet. I test it on a OnePlus 12, with a Pixel 5 as the helper phone.

Combining connections

With Combine connections on, your phone splits the stream across every connection it has. Your BRB server puts it back together and sends one stream to Twitch. Try a dead zone:

A simulation with made-up figures, in Mb/s. The real live screen shows each connection like this along the bottom.

With two or three connections carrying the stream, a weak spot on one matters less, and Fullbars lowers the quality as soon as things start to struggle. If every connection drops, say in a tunnel, the server keeps Twitch going on your Signal lost card for up to 10 minutes, and your picture comes back within seconds of getting signal. Nothing runs at home, so you don't need a PC or home internet.

What's in the app

Before you go live

Test streams

Test is the default. It uses Twitch's bandwidth test, so the stream reaches Twitch but nobody can watch it. There's a pre-flight check before you start, a preview before anything is sent, and a recap after, which reads like: "We kept you live through 3 connection drops."

Microphone

A live level meter, a sound check you can play back, a fader up to +30 dB, a limiter, and a switch for the phone's own auto level, which on some phones blows your voice out.

On stream

Scene cards

Starting soon, BRB, Privacy and Ending, one tap away, up to four scenes of your own, plus automatic Signal lost, Weak signal and Camera lost cards. Your phone draws them, so they look the same with or without the server.

Each card can have its own text in one of eight fonts, any colour, a picture, a still or GIF background, a layout and an accent. Add a countdown, a time-away counter, chat on the card, music under it, or a web page instead of the card.

Alerts

Follows, subs, gift subs, bits, raids and tips, drawn on stream with sound. Pick which ones show, where, how big and for how long, and give each kind your own words, picture, colours and sound. Your phone buzzes for each one, even with another app open, and the live screen shows who sent it.

Viewer TTS

A "Make me talk" channel points reward reads viewers' messages out on stream. Fullbars adds the reward to your channel and pauses it when you're not live, and points for anything that isn't said come back. Alerts can be said out loud too. For each kind you choose nothing, a thank-you ("Thank you Sam for the 500 bits!"), or the thank-you and their message, from a minimum you set.

It's all said on your phone in its one voice so far, a diva, and mixed into the stream without going through your mic. You hear it in your earbuds; on a Test stream without earbuds it plays from the speaker. The worst slurs, your own blocked words and your Twitch blocked terms are filtered out. Viewers' messages wait 3 seconds by default (you can pick 0, 5 or 10), so mods have time to type !skip, and you can skip from Scenes.

Chat and overlays

Twitch chat with emotes, on your screen and on stream. Chat read aloud into your earbuds. Text, image, or StreamElements and Streamlabs web overlays.

Live captions

What viewers hear you say, recognised on your phone and written on the stream. Nothing is sent anywhere, and they stop while you're muted. Android 13 or newer.

DJI Pocket 3 (unofficial, in Labs)

Fullbars starts the Pocket's stream over Bluetooth and takes it in over Wi‑Fi, so the Pocket works as a wireless camera on Android.

DJI doesn't support it, so the app asks you to confirm when you choose it in Settings › Camera. Other RTMP cameras work too.

Mod commands and remote

Mod chat commands, and a web remote your mods can open to switch scenes for you, your own scenes included, or skip a TTS message.

With your BRB server

Helper phones

A spare phone in your pocket adds its mobile data. While you're live it joins your streaming phone's hotspot by itself, so there's nothing to type.

Helpers speak Moblin's Moblink protocol, so Moblin's helpers should work too. So far I've only tested it with Fullbars on both phones.

Also stream to

Your BRB server can send the same stream to Kick, YouTube or any RTMP server too, up to three at once. Your phone still uploads it only once.

Chat, alerts and mod commands stay on Twitch. Test streams never go anywhere else.

My OBS

Streaming through OBS at home? The server can feed your OBS instead of Twitch, and NOALBS can switch scenes from its stats.

FAQ

Is it really free?

Yes. The app is free and open source (GPL-3.0). The only thing you might pay for is your BRB server, which you rent yourself from any provider, usually $4–10 a month. Oracle Cloud's free tier works too. Fullbars doesn't take a cut.

Do I need the BRB server?

No. Without one, Fullbars streams straight to Twitch over one connection, and scenes, chat, alerts and overlays all still work. The server is what lets you combine connections, use helper phones, and stay on air when everything drops.

What do viewers see when I lose signal?

With the BRB server, a Signal lost card ("The stream picks up when the connection's back") made by your phone to match your stream. Twitch sees one unbroken stream, and your picture comes back within seconds of your signal. If you're gone for 10 minutes, the server ends the stream.

Without the server, viewers see a frozen frame, and Twitch may show you as offline until your phone reconnects. If Disconnect Protection is on in your Twitch settings, Twitch can show its own fixed message for up to 90 seconds instead; I haven't tested that with Fullbars yet.

How much data does it use?

At 1080p and 6 Mb/s, about 2.8 GB an hour, split across your connections. Home shows the figure for the quality you pick before you go live. Fullbars lowers the quality on its own when the signal gets weak.

Is my stream key safe?

Sign in with Twitch and Fullbars fetches your key; you never copy it anywhere. With a BRB server, the phone sends it over an encrypted connection that trusts only your server's key, from the pairing QR code. The server keeps it in a file only it can read and never prints or logs it. Kick or YouTube keys you add for Also stream to are kept the same way. The stream itself is encrypted between your phone and your server.

Does the app collect anything?

No. There's no Fullbars account, and no analytics or crash reporting. The app talks only to Twitch, to StreamElements or Streamlabs if you add them, to web pages you add as overlays or cards, and to your own BRB server.
