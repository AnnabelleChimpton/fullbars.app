Android · Twitch · free and open source

Stream from your phone. Stay live in dead zones.

Fullbars is a free Android app for streaming IRL on Twitch. It can send your stream over mobile data, a Wi‑Fi hotspot and spare phones at once, through a small server you own. When every connection drops, viewers see a card instead of a frozen stream.

How it works01

Several connections in. One steady stream out.

With Combine connections on, your phone splits the stream across every connection it has. Your BRB server puts it back together and sends one stream to Twitch. Try a dead zone:

A simulation with made-up figures, in Mb/s. The real live screen shows each connection like this along the bottom.

  1. Weak spots hurt less

    Two or three connections carry the stream at once, and Fullbars lowers the quality before things stall, not after.

  2. Dropouts don't end your stream

    In a tunnel or a dead zone, the server keeps the Twitch stream going with a Signal lost card your phone made, for up to 10 minutes. When you're back, the picture returns within seconds.

  3. Nothing runs at home

    No PC and no home internet. The server is yours, so no streaming service sits in the middle, and it never writes your stream key to its logs.

What's in the app03

What's in the app

Test streams

Test is the default. It uses Twitch's bandwidth test, so nobody can watch a test on Twitch, not even you. A pre-flight check before you start, a preview before anything is sent, and a recap after: "We kept you live through 3 connection drops."

Helper phones

A spare phone in your pocket adds its mobile data. While you're live it joins your streaming phone's hotspot by itself, so there's nothing to type.

Helpers speak Moblin's Moblink protocol, so Moblin's helpers should work too. So far it's tested with Fullbars on both phones.

DJI Pocket 3 Labs

Fullbars starts the Pocket's stream over Bluetooth and takes it in over Wi‑Fi, so the Pocket works as a wireless camera on Android.

Unofficial: DJI doesn't support it, so the app asks you to confirm when you choose it in Settings › Camera. Other RTMP cameras work too.

Scene cards

Starting soon, BRB, Privacy and Ending, one tap away, up to four scenes of your own, plus automatic Signal lost, Weak signal and Camera lost cards. Your phone draws them, so they look the same with or without the server.

Make each one yours: your words in one of eight fonts, any colour, a picture, a still or GIF background, a layout and an accent. Add a countdown, a time-away counter, chat on the card, music under it, or a web page instead of the card.

Alerts

Follows, subs, gift subs, bits, raids and tips, drawn on stream with sound. Pick which ones show, where, how big and for how long, and give each kind your own words, picture, colours and sound. You feel each one land, and see who sent it.

Viewer TTS

A "Make me talk" channel points reward reads viewers' messages out on stream, and alerts can be said out loud too: "Thank you Sam for the 500 bits!" Spoken on your phone in one bright, dramatic voice, filtered for slurs and your own blocked words, and mods can !skip.

Live captions

What viewers hear you say, recognised on your phone and written on the stream. Nothing is sent anywhere, and they stop while you're muted. Android 13 or newer.

Your mic, your level

A live level meter, a sound check you can play back, a fader up to +30 dB, a limiter, and a switch for the phone's own auto level, which on some phones blows your voice out.

Chat and overlays

Twitch chat with emotes, on your screen and on stream. Chat read aloud into your earbuds. Text, image, or StreamElements and Streamlabs web overlays.

Also stream to

Your BRB server can send the same stream to Kick, YouTube or any RTMP server too, up to three at once. Your phone still uploads it only once.

Chat, alerts and mod commands stay on Twitch. Test streams never go anywhere else.

Your mods

Mod chat commands, and a web remote your mods can open to switch scenes for you, your own scenes included, or skip a TTS message.

My OBS

Streaming through OBS at home? The server can feed your OBS instead of Twitch, and NOALBS can switch scenes from its stats.

Let chat talk.

Viewers can make your stream speak: a channel points reward reads their message out loud, and alerts can thank people by name as they come in. It's all said on your phone, in one bright, dramatic voice, and mixed into the stream without going through your mic.

Make me talk

Fullbars adds the reward to your channel and pauses it whenever you're not live. Points for anything that isn't said come back.

Alerts said out loud

For each kind: nothing, a thank-you, or the thank-you and their message, from the size you choose. A thank-you plays while its alert is on screen.

Safe by default

The worst slurs, your own blocked words and your Twitch blocked terms are filtered out. Mods can type !skip, and you can skip from Scenes.

You hear it too

In your earbuds as viewers do. On a Test stream with no earbuds in, it plays from the phone's speaker.

Alerts you can't miss

A firm buzz for every alert, even with another app open, and who sent it on the live screen.

Captions that sit right

Lower on the picture, smaller, and with shorter lines, after feedback from viewers.

Questions05

FAQ

Is it really free?

Yes. The app is free and open source (GPL-3.0). The only thing you might pay for is your BRB server, which you rent yourself from any provider, usually $4–10 a month. Oracle Cloud's free tier works too. Fullbars doesn't take a cut or see your stream.

Which phones does it work on?

Android 12 or newer. So far it's been tested on a OnePlus 12 (Android 16) as the streaming phone, with a Pixel 5 (Android 14) as a helper. Other phones should work, but they haven't been tested yet. If yours misbehaves, please open an issue on GitHub.

Is there an iPhone version?

No, Fullbars is Android only.

Do I need the BRB server?

No. Without one, Fullbars streams straight to Twitch over one connection, and scenes, chat, alerts and overlays all still work. The server is what lets you combine connections, use helper phones, and stay on air when everything drops.

What do viewers see when I lose signal?

With the BRB server, a Signal lost card ("The stream picks up when the connection's back") made by your phone to match your stream. Twitch sees one unbroken stream, and your picture comes back within seconds of your signal. If you're gone for 10 minutes, the server ends the stream.

Without the server, viewers see a frozen frame, and Twitch may show you as offline until your phone reconnects. If Disconnect Protection is on in your Twitch settings, Twitch can show its own fixed message for up to 90 seconds instead; we haven't tested that with Fullbars yet.

How much data does it use?

At 1080p and 6 Mb/s, about 2.8 GB an hour, split across your connections. Home shows the figure for the quality you pick before you go live. Fullbars lowers the quality on its own when the signal gets weak.

Is my stream key safe?

Sign in with Twitch and Fullbars fetches your key; you never copy it anywhere. With a BRB server, the phone sends it over an encrypted connection that trusts only your server's key, from the pairing QR code. The server keeps it in a file only it can read and never prints or logs it. Kick or YouTube keys you add for Also stream to are kept the same way. The stream itself is encrypted between your phone and your server.

Does the DJI Pocket 3 work?

Yes, as a Labs feature. Choose DJI Pocket 3 in Settings › Camera and confirm, and Fullbars finds the Pocket over Bluetooth, starts its stream and takes it in over Wi‑Fi. It's unofficial: DJI doesn't support it, and a future Pocket firmware could break it. Any camera that can stream RTMP works too.

Can I stream to YouTube or Kick?

Yes, with your BRB server. Settings › Also stream to sends the same stream to Kick, YouTube or any RTMP server, up to three at once, next to Twitch. Your phone uploads it once; the server sends the copies. Chat, alerts and mod commands stay on Twitch, and Test streams never go anywhere else.

Without the server, Fullbars streams to Twitch only.

Does the app collect anything?

No. There's no account with us, and no analytics or crash reporting. The app talks only to Twitch, to StreamElements or Streamlabs if you add them, to web pages you add as overlays or cards, and to your own BRB server.

Is it on Google Play?

Not yet. Test builds are on GitHub. They're debug-signed, so when a release-signed build arrives you'll need to uninstall the test build first.
